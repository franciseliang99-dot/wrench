# M-1 Overview

## M-1.1 What the WD-200 does

The WD-200 supplies compressed air to the brakes, doors and suspension of a passenger train. An electric motor drives a screw
compressor; the air passes a twin-tower desiccant dryer and is stored in the main reservoirs. The unit runs in cycles: it loads
until the reservoirs reach the upper setpoint, then unloads and idles until pressure falls to the lower setpoint.

## M-1.2 Normal load cycle

A healthy unit loads when reservoir pressure falls to about 8.2 bar and unloads at about 10.2 bar. While idle, pressure should fall
slowly, because only the train's consumers draw air. Typical idle periods last many minutes. The motor draws about 4 A when
unloaded, about 7 A under load and briefly up to 9 A at start. The two dryer towers alternate on a fixed timer.

## M-1.3 Safety before any work

Never open a pressurised circuit. Before touching the unit, isolate it electrically, vent the reservoirs and confirm zero pressure
on the gauge (see M-6.3). Hot oil can cause burns for up to 30 minutes after the unit stops. Hearing protection is required near a
running compressor.
